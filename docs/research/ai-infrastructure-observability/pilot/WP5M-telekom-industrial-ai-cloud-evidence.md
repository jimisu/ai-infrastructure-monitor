## Identity evidence and unresolved relationships

- Candidate physical scope: the renovated data center at Munich Tucherpark containing the Industrial AI Cloud/AI factory infrastructure.
- Deutsche Telekom/T-Systems provides infrastructure/platform; NVIDIA supplies compute technology; Polarise is identified as data-center partner; SAP and customers belong to the service/application ecosystem.
- “Industrial AI Cloud”, “AI factory”, “Deutschland Stack”, the physical renovated data center, and customer workloads are related layers, not automatically separate projects or additive capacities.
- German and English announcements describe the same deployment and must not be counted as independent project evidence.

## Milestone and date evidence

- D1 is an announcement with a completion target by 2026.
- D2 supports installation underway and a Q1 2026 planned go-live, not commissioning at publication.
- D3 explicitly announces official launch on 2026-02-04 and says the factory was operating above one third of capacity with customers.
- “Built over six months” must not be transformed into an invented construction-start date.

## AI attribution and capacity evidence

- All operator releases explicitly describe infrastructure for AI training, inference, industrial AI, simulation, robotics, and sovereign AI workloads.
- D1 states 10,000 chips/GPUs prospectively; D2 says up to 10,000 while installation was underway; D3 says nearly 10,000 at launch.
- Those phrases have different threshold and lifecycle meanings. They must not be silently normalized to exactly 10,000 installed or commissioned accelerators.

- “Operating above one third of capacity” is utilization/service wording, not proof that a numeric fraction of GPUs was commissioned unless the source explicitly binds it.
