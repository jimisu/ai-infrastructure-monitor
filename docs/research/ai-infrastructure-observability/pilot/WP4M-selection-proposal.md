## 1. Boundary and method

This is a shallow, bounded feasibility screen, not case research. The screen observed whether public
official pages were available and noted access restrictions plus the expected project/phase identity,
AI-attribution and capacity-basis questions. It did not download or preserve source snapshots, extract
complete claims, create source/version or project/phase dossiers, or assign final lifecycle or capacity
classifications. The screen was completed within the authorized two-hour ceiling; exact minutes were
not instrumented and are not reconstructed.

All pages below were reachable as public HTML or a public PDF during the screen without sign-in or
payment. That observation is not permission for automated acquisition. Robots directives, website
terms, copyright/reuse rights, stable-version behavior and any jurisdiction-specific restrictions were
not assessed. Those remain access/legal unknowns to resolve before any separately authorized WP5M
acquisition.

## 2. Proposed depth cases — exactly two

### Case 1 — xAI Colossus, Memphis, Tennessee, United States

- **Why select:** evidence-rich operator and supplier channels are publicly available; the operator
  explicitly connects the named Memphis system to AI model training and publishes accelerator-scale
  statements. This makes it a strong test of accelerator-based capacity and rapid phase expansion.
- **Official-source availability observed:** [xAI Colossus](https://x.ai/colossus),
  [xAI Memphis](https://x.ai/memphis), and
  [NVIDIA investor release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2024/xAI-Colossus-NVIDIA-Spectrum-X-Ethernet-Networking-Platform-and-NVIDIA-Hopper-GPUs-Power-Worlds-Largest-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx).
- **Access restrictions / legal unknowns:** public HTML was reachable without authentication;
  automated-access terms, robots treatment, quotation/reuse rights and durable version locators remain
  unreviewed.
- **Expected identity challenge:** distinguish the named Colossus system from the Memphis campus,
  later expansion phases and supplier descriptions of the same deployment; prevent accelerator totals
  from being added across repeated announcements.
- **AI-attribution feasibility:** high at screen level because the operator names AI training use; the
  dossier would still have to bind each phase and capacity statement to the same project identity.
- **Capacity-basis feasibility:** high at screen level for physical accelerator counts; no equivalence
  conversion is proposed. Facility power, future expansion and installed/operational counts must remain
  separate if research is later authorized.

### Case 2 — Deutsche Telekom Industrial AI Cloud, Munich, Germany

- **Why select:** supplies the sovereign-AI / lower-English-accessibility dimension, while official
  German and English operator pages appear sufficient to test language parity, renovated-site identity
  and a threshold-scale accelerator statement without relying on a media-only source.
- **Official-source availability observed:**
  [German launch page](https://www.telekom.com/de/medien/medieninformationen/detail/deutsche-telekom-und-nvidia-eroeffnen-industrial-ai-cloud-1099688),
  [English launch page](https://www.telekom.com/en/media/media-information/archive/deutsche-telekom-and-nvidia-launch-industrial-ai-cloud-1099700), and
  [English operations page](https://www.telekom.com/en/media/media-information/archive/deutsche-telekom-opens-industrial-ai-cloud-1099866).
- **Access restrictions / legal unknowns:** public HTML was reachable without authentication; German
  and English pages may not be exact semantic mirrors. Automated-access terms, robots treatment,
  translation/reuse rights and stable versioning remain unreviewed.
- **Expected identity challenge:** distinguish the Munich data-center renovation, the Industrial AI
  Cloud service, the underlying AI factory/cluster and launch versus operating milestones; avoid
  treating bilingual or partner announcements as independent projects.
- **AI-attribution feasibility:** high at screen level because operator pages frame the infrastructure
  as an industrial AI cloud/factory; later research must determine which physical scope each statement
  actually covers.
- **Capacity-basis feasibility:** promising for a stated physical GPU count. Installation, availability,
  service capacity and any performance-equivalent measures must not be conflated.

The recommendation is to approve both cases together. The second case is not a fallback of reduced
quality: it has accessible official channels and exposes the intended sovereign/language and identity
stress. Final eligibility remains unclassified until authorized dossier work and independent review.

## 4. Exclusions and stop conditions

- No pool candidate was promoted to replace either proposed case because doing so would reduce the
  intended contrast or introduce a broader unresolved program/site/phase identity problem.
- No candidate was rejected as ineligible. “Discovery-only” is a scope decision, not a lifecycle,
  AI-attribution or capacity classification.
- Supplier, operator, customer and government pages that describe the same physical deployment must
  not be counted as independent projects merely because they are separate publications.
- Any inaccessible primary page, ambiguous acquisition terms, unsupported AI share, capacity-basis
  substitution, invented date precision or unresolved project/phase identity is a stop or conflict to
  preserve during later research.
